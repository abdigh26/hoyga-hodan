01WHO WE ARE

Culture, commerce
& confidence.

BelongingVisibilityJoy

Hoyga Hodan is a Somali women-led space where women come together to learn, connect, create and thrive. We combine practical skills and entrepreneurship learning with community, culture, creativity and opportunities to build relationships.

The experience is designed around what participants value: a welcoming place to meet new people, exchange ideas, explore creativity, take part in cultural activities and leave with greater confidence.

Hoyga Hodan is not simply a training workshop. Each gathering combines practical learning with structured community and cultural activities that encourage participation, connection and belonging. Traditional games, competitions, art, music, karaoke, networking and social connection are part of the model—not an afterthought.

Participants repeatedly ask for the gatherings to happen again and ask Hoyga Hodan to bring the experience to their cities. That demand for learning, joy and connection is central evidence for the model.

When women have room to learn, share, enjoy, and be seen, they can build confidence and relationships that may open the door to future opportunity.

Our approach

02OUR STORY

Four gatherings made one thing clear.

Somali women want spaces that make it possible to learn, meet, create, celebrate and return.

A COMMUNITY-LED START

Built from many kinds of support

The first gatherings were made possible through $10 participant contributions, local businesses, donated services, free food and water, volunteers, discounted or free media, occasional financial support, and determined founder and team effort.

FOUR GATHERINGS

Approximately 600 women reached

Across Eco Hub, Muniiza Hall, Dhaqan Gooob and Baraawe, Hoyga Hodan reconstructed participation at approximately 600 women—422 paid participants and 178 free participants.

BARAAWE

A signal of participant demand

The Baraawe gathering created strong happiness and connection among women participants, who repeatedly asked for more gatherings and for Hoyga Hodan to bring the experience to their cities. It remains an important example of why the model should continue and grow carefully.

NEXT STAGE

From unpredictable support to strategic partnership

Community support proved there is demand. Hoyga Hodan is now seeking more predictable institutional and strategic partnerships so future gatherings can be planned, delivered and measured with care.

Four engines of empowerment.

Gatherings That Build Belonging

Each Hoyga Hodan gathering combines practical learning with structured community and cultural activities that encourage participation, confidence, connection and belonging. Depending on the gathering, the experience can include:

09OUR CURRENT TEAM

Founder / Director

1 role · unpaid

02

Programme & Operations

1 role · unpaid

Finance & Trainers

1 finance role + 2 trainers · unpaid

Developer + media paid · 3–5 volunteers unpaid

Building capacity carefully → With institutional support, Hoyga Hodan aims to strengthen programme management, finance and administration, monitoring and evaluation, and communications.

From community effort to strategic support.

The first four gatherings were made possible by $10 participant contributions alongside local businesses, donated services, free food and water, volunteer trainers and staff, discounted or free media, occasional financial support, and founder/team effort. That effort created momentum, but it is difficult to secure consistently. Hoyga Hodan is moving from ad-hoc community support toward strategic fundraising and is now seeking predictable institutional and strategic partners to support the next stage.

12WHAT WE WILL MEASURE NEXT

Growth should be felt—and measured.

The first four gatherings show participation and demand. As support grows, Hoyga Hodan plans to build a simple, practical learning and impact framework rather than claim outcomes it has not yet measured.

To be measured

Participation

Attendance, completion and the mix of paid and free places.

To be measured

Experience

Participant satisfaction, confidence and the quality of the gathering experience.

To be measured

Learning

Skills participants say they gained or want to keep developing.

To be measured

Connection

New peer, mentor and partner connections made through the programme.

To be measured

Action

Business or personal action plans that participants choose to pursue.

To be measured

Economic Pathways

Businesses started or improved and other economic outcomes where participants report them.

13HOW WE WANT TO GROW

Warm, local and accountable.

01

Participant-Centred

Keep women’s experience, dignity, participation and connection at the centre of every gathering.

02

Locally Grounded

Build with Somali culture, local partnerships and the realities participants describe—not a copied model.

03

Transparent About Growth

Separate what has happened from what is planned, and avoid claiming outcomes before they are measured.

04

Open to Partnership

Welcome practical partners who can help turn community demand into reliable, well-supported gatherings.
